## 1. Basic Terminology

### Chanda (Metre)
- Derived from root **chad** meaning "that which delights"
- A fixed and balanced system of measures of sound (mātrā)
- The ordered measures of the poetic spirit

### Padya (Verse)
- Any metrical composition in Sanskrit
- Generally contains four **pādas** (quarters/metrical lines)
- First two pādas form the first half; remaining two form the last half

### Pāda (Quarter)
- Each of the four lines of a verse
- Regulated either by:
  - Number of syllables (**akṣara**)
  - Number of syllabic instants (**mātrā**)

### Akṣara (Syllable)
- As much of a word as can be pronounced distinctly at one effort of the voice
- A vowel with or without one or more consonants = one syllable

## 2. Syllable Classification Rules

### Laghu (Short Syllables) - Symbol: `u` or `~`
Short vowels that create short syllables:
- **अ** (a)
- **इ** (i)
- **उ** (u)
- **ऋ** (ṛ)
- **लृ** (ḷ)

Examples: क (ka), कि (ki) are short syllables.

### Guru (Long Syllables) - Symbol: `-`
Long vowels that create long syllables:
- **आ** (ā)
- **ई** (ī)
- **ऊ** (ū)
- **ॠ** (ṝ)
- **ए** (e)
- **ऐ** (ai)
- **ओ** (o)
- **औ** (au)

Examples: का (kā), की (kī) are long syllables.

### Rules for Short Becoming Long
A short vowel becomes **long (guru)** under three conditions:

1. **Anusvāra Rule**: If followed by an anusvāra (ṁ)
   - Examples: कं (kaṁ), गं (gaṁ)

2. **Visarga Rule**: If followed by a visarga (ḥ)
   - Examples: कः (kaḥ), गः (gaḥ)

3. **Conjunct Consonant Rule**: If followed by a conjunct consonant
   - Example: In गन्ध (gandha), **ग** (ga) is counted as long because it's followed by न्ध (ndha)
   - Count as: गन् (gan) + ध (dha)

### End-of-Quarter Rule
At the end of a quarter:
- A short syllable **can be considered long**
- A long syllable **can be considered short**
- As per the requirement of the chanda

## 3. Gaṇa (Syllabic Feet) System

### Definition
- Each metrical line is divided into groups of **three syllables** each
- Each group is called a **gaṇa** or syllabic foot
- Distinguished by particular arrangement of syllables

### The Eight Gaṇas

| Gaṇa | Name | Pattern | Description |
|------|------|---------|-------------|
| **न** | na gaṇa | `uuu` | All three syllables short |
| **य** | ya gaṇa | `u--` | 1st short, 2nd & 3rd long |
| **र** | ra gaṇa | `-u-` | Middle short, others long |
| **त** | ta gaṇa | `--u` | Last short, others long |
| **म** | ma gaṇa | `---` | All three syllables long |
| **भ** | bha gaṇa | `-uu` | 1st long, others short |
| **ज** | ja gaṇa | `u-u` | Middle long, others short |
| **स** | sa gaṇa | `uu-` | Last long, others short |

### Additional Single-Syllable Gaṇas
| Symbol | Name | Pattern |
|--------|------|---------|
| **ग** | ga | `-` (single long) |
| **ल** | la | `u` (single short) |

### Mnemonic for Gaṇas
The traditional mnemonic: **य-मा-ता-रा-ज-भा-न-स-ल-गम्** (yamātārājabhānasalagam)

## 4. Verse Classification

### Main Categories

#### 4.1 Vṛttachanda (Syllable-regulated)
Regulated by number and position of syllables in each pāda.

**Three sub-categories:**

| Type | Sanskrit | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| **Samavṛtta** | समवृत्त | Even metres - all four quarters identical |
| **Ardhasamavṛtta** | अर्धसमवृत्त | Half-even - alternate quarters same (1st=3rd, 2nd=4th) |
| **Viṣamavṛtta** | विषमवृत्त | Uneven - all quarters different |

#### 4.2 Jātivṛtta / Mātrāvṛtta
Regulated by **mātrās** (syllabic instants), not syllable count.

## 5. Yati (Metrical Pause) Rules

### Types of Pause

#### Sentential Pause
- Occurs at **end of hemistich** (middle of verse): marked by `|` (single line)
- Occurs at **end of verse**: marked by `||` (double line)

#### Harmonic Pause (Caesura)
- Occurs after specific syllables **within** a quarter
- Varies from metre to metre
- Examples:
  - **Śikhariṇī**: pause after 6th and 17th syllables
  - **Śārdūlavikrīḍitam**: pause after 12th and 19th syllables

| **अतिधृतिः** (Atidhṛtiḥ) | 76 |

### Vedic vs Classical Metre Rules
- **Vedic**: Syllable COUNT matters; arrangement can vary
- **Classical**: Both COUNT and ARRANGEMENT of short/long syllables matter
- **Vedic tolerance**: 1-2 syllables more or less is acceptable (not chandobhaṅga)
- **Classical**: Any deviation is chandobhaṅga (breaking the metre)

## 10. Mātrā Chandas (Syllabic Instant Metres)

### Mātrā Definition
- **1 mātrā** = time to utter a short vowel
- **Short vowels** = 1 mātrā
- **Long vowels/diphthongs** = 2 mātrās

Example:
- तप (tapa) = 2 mātrās (1+1)
- ताप (tāpa) = 3 mātrās (2+1)

### Major Divisions
1. **Āryā** - Most common
2. **Vaitālīya**
3. **Mātrāsamaka**

### Āryā Varieties (9 types)

| Name | Pattern (mātrās per quarter) |
|------|------------------------------|
| **पथ्या** (Pathyā) | Standard āryā |
| **विपुला** (Vipulā) | - |
| **चपला** (Capalā) | - |
| **मुखचपला** (Mukhacapalā) | - |
| **जघनचपला** (Jaghanacapalā) | - |
| **गीति** (Gīti) | 12-18-12-18 |
| **उपगीति** (Upagīti) | 12-15-12-15 |
| **उद्गीति** (Udgīti) | 12-15-12-18 |
| **आर्यागीति** (Āryāgīti) | 12-20-12-20 |

### Āryā Base Pattern
- 1st quarter: 12 mātrās
- 2nd quarter: 18 mātrās
- 3rd quarter: 12 mātrās
- 4th quarter: 15 mātrās

### Vaitālīya Pattern
- 1st & 3rd lines: 14 mātrās (6 mātrās + `-u-u-`)
- 2nd & 4th lines: 16 mātrās (8 mātrās + `-u-u-`)

### Aupachandasika Pattern
- Like Vaitālīya but each line has one extra long syllable at end
- 1st & 3rd lines: 16 mātrās
- 2nd & 4th lines: 18 mātrās

### Mātrāsamaka Pattern
- Each quarter: 16 mātrās
- 9th mātrā: short
- Last mātrā: long

## Chandobhaṅga (Breaking the Metre)

### Classical Sanskrit Rule
If any quarter has one or two syllables **more or less** than prescribed:
- The metre is considered **defective**
- This is called **chandobhaṅga** (breaking the rules)
- **Not permitted** by prosodians

### Vedic Exception
In Vedic metres, 1-2 syllables more or less:
- **Not considered defective**
- Does not change the chanda to another
- Special terms used:
  - **निचृद्** (nicṛd): 1 syllable less
  - **विराट्** (virāṭ): 2 syllables less
  - **भूरिक्** (bhūrik): 1 syllable more
  - **स्वराट्** (svarāṭ): 2 syllables more

## Summary of Key Rules

1. **Short syllables (laghu)**: a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ → 1 mātrā
2. **Long syllables (guru)**: ā, ī, ū, ṝ, e, ai, o, au → 2 mātrās
3. **Short becomes long**: Before anusvāra, visarga, or conjunct
4. **End of quarter**: Short/long can interchange as needed
5. **Gaṇas**: 8 three-syllable groups + 2 single-syllable groups
6. **Samavṛtta**: All 4 quarters identical
7. **Ardhasamavṛtta**: 1st=3rd, 2nd=4th
8. **Viṣamavṛtta**: All 4 quarters different
9. **Mātrāvṛtta**: Regulated by mātrā count, not syllables
10. **Yati**: Sentential (|, ||) and Harmonic (caesura) pauses
