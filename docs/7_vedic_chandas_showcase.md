## 📚 Understanding Laghu (ल) and Guru (ग)

### What makes a syllable **Laghu (Short)** - ल / L?

A syllable is **Laghu** when it contains:
- A **short vowel** (अ, इ, उ, ऋ, ऌ)
- NOT followed by a conjunct consonant

### What makes a syllable **Guru (Long)** - ग / G?

A syllable is **Guru** when it contains:
- A **long vowel** (आ, ई, ऊ, ॠ, ए, ऐ, ओ, औ)
- OR a short vowel followed by **anusvāra** (ं)
- OR a short vowel followed by **visarga** (ः)
- OR a short vowel followed by a **conjunct consonant**
- OR the **final syllable** of a pāda (by position)

## 🧮 The Meru Prastāra Pattern

Notice the beautiful mathematical pattern in the 7 Vedic meters:

```
Gāyatrī:     24 = 3 × 8   (+4 from previous)
Uṣṇik:       28 = 4 × 7   (+4)
Anuṣṭubh:    32 = 4 × 8   (+4)
Bṛhatī:      36 = 4 × 9   (+4)
Paṅkti:      40 = 5 × 8   (+4)
Triṣṭubh:    44 = 4 × 11  (+4)
Jagatī:      48 = 4 × 12  (+4)
```

**Each meter adds exactly 4 syllables!**

This is the wisdom of **Meru Prastāra** (मेरु प्रस्तार) - the ancient Indian discovery that predates Pascal's Triangle by 1,500 years!
